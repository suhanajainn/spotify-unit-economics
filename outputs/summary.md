# Spotify unit economics: key findings

Generated by `scripts/model.py` from `data/quarterly_metrics.csv` and `assumptions.md`.
Quarterly detail is in `outputs/unit_economics.csv`. Data runs through Q2 2026.

Comparisons use the latest four quarters (Q3 2025–Q2 2026) against the four before (Q3 2024–Q2 2025), not
single quarters, to smooth out seasonality, rounding and one-off charges.

## Bottom line

1. **Premium is more profitable per subscriber. This is reported data.** Gross profit per
   subscriber per month averaged €1.63 over Q3 2025–Q2 2026, up from €1.59, because Premium
   gross margin rose from 33.7% to 34.4%.
2. **Premium gross profit covered all sales and marketing spend 3.8x** over Q3 2025–Q2 2026,
   up from 3.6x. Also reported data, and it's most of what the LTV:CAC figure below
   is really measuring.
3. **LTV:CAC of 4.5x–5.1x and payback of
   5.6–9.8 months are estimates.** Payback depends heavily on the
   churn assumption; LTV:CAC hardly does (see section 2).
4. **Marketing efficiency didn't clearly improve, and reported S&M likely understates the latest
   year.** Reported S&M rose 5.5% while subscribers grew, so S&M per subscriber
   edged down. But Social Charges (payroll taxes on stock compensation, driven by the share price)
   added €340M to operating expenses over Q3 2024–Q2 2025 and were a net credit of €104M
   over Q3 2025–Q2 2026. Spotify doesn't say how much of that sits in S&M; whatever share does means
   underlying S&M grew faster than reported.

## 1. Premium profitability (reported data)

| | Q3 2024–Q2 2025 | Q3 2025–Q2 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Premium gross margin (avg) | 33.7% | 34.4% |
| Premium ARPU (avg) | €4.71 | €4.72 |
| Gross profit per subscriber per month (avg) | €1.59 | €1.63 |
| Premium gross profit (total) | €4,987M | €5,627M |
| Sales and marketing (total) | €1,403M | €1,480M |

- The gain came from **margin**. Euro ARPU rose 0.1% between the two
  periods, while constant-currency ARPU growth averaged +3.8% Y/Y over Q3 2025–Q2 2026
  (as stated in the decks). The euro weakness in 2025 was mostly exchange rates, not pricing.
- Premium gross profit here is revenue × the margin rounded to one decimal, so it can differ from
  Spotify's own segment gross profit by a few million euros.

## 2. Acquisition cost, LTV and payback (estimates)

These depend on assumed monthly churn, which Spotify doesn't report (see `assumptions.md`).
Over Q3 2025–Q2 2026:

| Churn case | CAC | LTV | LTV:CAC | Payback (months) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low (2.0%) | €15.89 | €81 | 5.1x | 9.8 |
| Base (3.0%) | €11.59 | €54 | 4.7x | 7.1 |
| High (4.0%) | €9.12 | €41 | 4.5x | 5.6 |

How to read this:

- **LTV:CAC barely moves with churn because it's mostly a reported ratio in disguise.** With our
  formulas, LTV:CAC ≈ Premium gross profit ÷ S&M (3.8x) plus a term for net subscriber
  growth. Higher churn shortens lifetime but raises the implied number of gross adds, and the two
  almost cancel. The base-case 4.7x is no more precise than "Premium gross profit covers
  S&M about 4 times, plus credit for growth".
- **Payback is where churn matters.** It ranges 5.6–9.8 months across the cases.
- **A falling CAC is mostly arithmetic.** In the base case 81% of estimated gross adds are
  replacements for assumed churn, and assumed churn grows with the subscriber base. If S&M is flat,
  CAC falls automatically as the base grows. Base-case CAC went from €11.32 (Q3 2024–Q2 2025) to
  €11.59 (Q3 2025–Q2 2026). The assumption-free equivalent is **S&M per average Premium
  subscriber per month: €0.45, then €0.43**.
- **LTV is undiscounted** and assumes today's ARPU and margin for the whole lifetime. With constant
  churn, LTV is just gross profit per subscriber × 33 months; its trend adds no
  information beyond section 1.

### Sales and marketing is distorted in several quarters

Reported S&M includes one-off and share-price-driven charges that aren't acquisition spend.
Spotify discloses them only for operating expenses as a whole, so we can't remove them from S&M
without guessing. We leave S&M as reported and flag the quarters instead.

Social Charges by quarter (negative = credit). The `sm_distortion_flag` column in the CSV marks
quarters where they're €50M or more either way.

| Quarter | S&M (€M) | Social Charges in all operating expenses (€M) |
|---|---|---|
| Q3 2023 | 355 | +1 |
| Q4 2023 | 432 | +33 |
| Q1 2024 | 324 | +82 |
| Q2 2024 | 343 | +58 |
| Q3 2024 | 332 | +53 |
| Q4 2024 | 393 | +97 |
| Q1 2025 | 314 | +75 |
| Q2 2025 | 364 | +115 |
| Q3 2025 | 349 | -16 |
| Q4 2025 | 399 | -50 |
| Q1 2026 | 342 | -39 |
| Q2 2026 | 390 | +1 |

One-off charges:
- **Q4 2023:** €143M net severance and real-estate charges in operating expenses (Q4 2023 deck); split by line not disclosed.

Consequences:
- Trends that start at Q4 2023 (base-case CAC €14.03, the highest in the series) overstate the
  improvement, because that quarter carries severance charges.
- Comparing Q3 2025–Q2 2026 with Q3 2024–Q2 2025 flatters the latest period: operating expenses swung by
  €444M from Social Charges alone. Any share of that in S&M means underlying S&M,
  CAC and payback are higher in the latest period than shown.

## 3. Ad-supported (reported data)

- **Ad-Supported revenue per ad-supported user per month was €0.30 in Q2 2026,
  down from €0.38 in Q2 2024** (same quarter, to avoid seasonality). Ad-supported
  users grew faster than ad revenue.
- It's about 7% of Premium ARPU (average over Q3 2025–Q2 2026).
- Seasonal: Q4 was the highest quarter (€0.42, €0.37) and Q1 the lowest
  (€0.33, €0.32, €0.27) in each year we have. Only 2 Q4s are on the restated
  basis, so treat the pattern as indicative.
- **This overstates revenue per ad-supported user.** The Ad-Supported segment includes podcast
  advertising, and podcast ads also reach Premium subscribers, but we divide by ad-supported
  users only.
- We have no data on how many ad-supported users convert to Premium, so this analysis can't
  value the free tier as a pipeline.

## What this analysis can't tell you

- **Whether retention improved or worsened.** Churn is an assumption held constant.
- **True Premium acquisition cost.** S&M also funds ad-supported growth, brand and the ad sales team,
  and includes the charges above. Our CAC overstates Premium CAC, on a noisy base.
- **Q3 2023 acquisition cost**, because the Q2 2023 subscriber count isn't in our data, and **2023
  ad revenue per user**, because Spotify hasn't published restated 2023 segment figures.
- Subscriber counts are reported in whole millions, so single-quarter net adds (3–11M) carry up to
  ±1M of rounding.
